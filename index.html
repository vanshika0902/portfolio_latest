Automating how XGBoost models are built, cutting model-to-market time by 40%

American Express · 2024 to 2026 · Google Cloud, XGBoost, SHAP

40%less model-to-market time
~$25Mfinancial impact

Problem

Building and onboarding XGBoost models involved several separate steps: choosing variables, tuning hyperparameters, checking interpretability and writing reports. The goal was to bring these into one automated framework so models reach production faster.

Approach

I built an automated modeling framework on Google Cloud for XGBoost models. It combines modules for variable selection, hyperparameter tuning, model interpretability and reporting, and it supported onboarding models from other teams.

Related work on the same platform

  • Alert remediation: a GPU-enabled root-cause analysis process that explains alerts raised during interpretability checks.
  • Data stability: a module that detects data drift, concept drift and label drift using a combination of metrics.
  • Data Shapley: used to find influential data points and improve dataset selection.

What I learned

Automation only helps when each step still produces something people can inspect, so interpretability and reporting were part of the framework from the start.

Moving analytical workloads to GCP and speeding up processing by 94%

American Express · 2022 to 2024 · GCP, RAPIDS, Dask

94%faster processing
300credit-risk and fraud teams
~$60Mcumulative impact

Problem

Analytical modeling and data-processing workloads ran on on-premises infrastructure and AWS. The goal was to consolidate them on GCP and make processing faster for the credit-risk and fraud teams that depend on them.

Approach

I led the enterprise-wide migration of modeling and data-processing workloads from on-premises infrastructure and AWS to GCP. I designed scalable data pipelines and implemented hyperparameter tuning and model-building frameworks using RAPIDS and Dask.

What I learned

Deutsche Bank · 2020 to 2022 · ETL, microservices, Tableau

£500ksavings

Problem

Financial reconciliations ran on third-party platforms. The goal was to move that work into an in-house ETL microservice and give stakeholders a clear view of the results.

Approach

I developed features for an ETL microservice that handles financial reconciliation, which allowed the company to decommission third-party platforms. I also built a Tableau dashboard so stakeholders could see the results.

What I learned

Predicting completion times for 10,000 books and cutting support incidents by 30%

Deutsche Bank · 2020 to 2022 · Supervised learning

10,000books covered
30%fewer support incidents

Problem

Users of the profit and loss reporting platform had no estimate of when each of 10,000 books would arrive, and the support team was raising incidents about it. The goal was to predict arrival times so users knew what to expect.

Approach

I led the design and development of an ML application that uses supervised learning to predict the estimated time of arrival for each book on the platform. It improved the user experience and reduced incidents raised by the support team.

What I learned

A prediction is more useful when it answers a question users are already asking, which here was when each book would arrive.
